--- title: Review by scholars description: How scholars with no tie to the project review History Globe, what a review means, and how it is published. ---

History Globe is written by one person with AI as a working partner and checked against its sources (see Methodology). That is not the same as being read by someone who knows the field. So we ask scholars with no tie to the project to read parts of it and tell us what is wrong, and we publish what they say.

What a review is here

A scholar reads a set of articles, answered questions or tours that we agree on together, in the version of a given date, and writes what is wrong, missing or doubtful. We answer every point, correct the text in all languages, and publish the review, our response and what changed. A review covers only the items listed with it and only the aspects named in its scope: “reviewed for …; not reviewed for …”.

Each reviewed article, question or tour then says so under its title, with the reviewer’s name, the institution and the date, and links to the review’s own page. The pages of articles and answers no scholar has read say that too.

What a review is not

It is not an endorsement of History Globe as a whole, of its other articles, or of any view on faith. It is not given on behalf of the reviewer’s university or employer; affiliations are shown as they were at the time of the review, for identification only.

Independence

Reviewers are not paid and pay nothing. They do not edit the site and have no say over it beyond their review. We ask every reviewer to declare any conflict of interest (for example, being an author of a source the articles rely on, or working with the author); the declaration is published with the review, even when it is “none”.

Consent

Nothing is published without the reviewer’s written approval of the exact public text: name, title, institution, scope, statement and whether the full review is public. We keep the date of that approval and a fingerprint of the text approved; the letter itself stays private. Reviews are published under the reviewer’s name; a scholar who would rather not be named is free to decline. Before publication we never mention a reviewer’s name to anyone.

Our response and corrections

We answer every point within two weeks: corrected, partly corrected, or not corrected and why. The reviewer sees our response before publication and may tell us a point is not addressed. Each correction is made in every language and recorded with its date and the change in the public repository. A review whose corrections are not finished says “corrections in progress”.

Versions

A review refers to the version it read, by its date and its commit in the public repository. If an item changes after the review, other than by the corrections the review asked for, its page says “changed since”, and the review’s page links the version read and the difference.

Negative reviews

We publish every review that its author allows us to publish, whatever it says. An item is not marked as reviewed in a way that hides what the review found.

Withdrawal

Before publication a reviewer may withdraw at any time, without giving a reason. After publication we take the review off the site at the reviewer’s request (reviews are not part of the API or the dataset). Earlier versions stay in the public history of the repository, as every change does; the change that removes the review says “a review was withdrawn at the reviewer’s request”. A DOI record on Zenodo, if the reviewer chose one, cannot be deleted; we say so before it is created.

Licence and credit

Reviewers keep the copyright of their reviews. Published reviews are licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0), unless the reviewer chooses to publish only a short statement. This differs from the licence of our own articles. If the reviewer wishes, the review is also deposited on Zenodo as a peer review with its own DOI, the reviewer as its author; the reviewer can add it to their ORCID record. Each review page shows how to cite it.

Translations

Reviewers read the English original. Their words are shown as written, in the language they wrote them in, on every language’s page; any translation of them is ours, shown beside the original and marked as such. A translation of a reviewed article has not itself been reviewed unless its page says so.

Use of AI

The articles were drafted with AI under the author’s direction and checked against their sources in AI-assisted passes; no scholar had read them before these reviews. Reviews are written by the reviewers alone, and we do not add to their words. Where we draft the public summary of a review from the reviewer’s letter, the reviewer approves or rewrites it before anything is published. Our responses, and our translations of them and of the reviewers’ words, are made with AI help.

Contact

If you would read a set of articles in your field, or have a question about a review, write to historyglobe.vadym@gmail.com. A reply in any form is welcome: a few lines in an email, notes on a PDF, or tracked changes in a document.
